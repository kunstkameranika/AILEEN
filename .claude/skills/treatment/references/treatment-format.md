# Treatment format and market package

## What a 10–15 page treatment is
It is a selling and development document. It tells the **whole story, ending
included**, in vivid present-tense prose, so that a producer, fund reader,
broadcaster or commissioner can judge the story, its tone, and whether it's worth making.
It is not a screenplay and not a scene list. It is also not a mood piece.

(McKee would call a document this short an outline with prose on it. That is why the
outline under it must pass the Gauntlet first. See `mckee.md`.)

Before writing, confirm any **spec the recipient sets**: funds, labs and broadcasters often
fix page counts, font, spacing, language, and whether a logline, synopsis or director's
statement goes alongside. Their spec beats these defaults.

## Defaults
- **Length:** 10–15 pages. Single-spaced 12pt is about 450–500 words a page, so about 4,500–7,500
  words in total. If the recipient wants 1.5 spacing, the word count falls to match.
- **Voice:** present tense, third person, active verbs. It's prose, not bullets.
- **Characters:** NAME IN CAPS on first appearance, with age and a one-line
  essence that shows *character through behaviour*, not adjectives.
- **Dialogue:** none, or at most a few quoted lines that land a turn.
- **Headings:** optional sequence or act headings (e.g. `I. THE FALL`) help
  navigation. Avoid slug lines.
- **Camera:** no shot directions. Use images, not lenses.
- **Ending:** told in full.

## Page budget (feature, 12 pp example)
| Block | Share | Pages | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening + world | ~8% | 1 | hook image, status quo, protagonist's "future", unresolved issue |
| Inciting → Act 1 break | ~17% | 2 | the incident, dithering, the antagonist aria / mentor, the decision |
| Act 2a → midpoint | ~25% | 3 | the plan, the plan blown up, promise-of-premise set-pieces, the midpoint reversal |
| Midpoint → Act 2 break | ~25% | 3 | deeper stakes, opponent pressing, local goal achieved → global stakes forced |
| Act 3 run → climax | ~20% | 2.5 | setbacks on all three stakes, Judas, kamikaze, despair, decisive act |
| Resolution | ~5% | 0.5 | new equilibrium, final image |

Scale the shares for 10 or 15 pages. Allot roughly 1–2 pages per Frank Daniel sequence.

## Writing it
1. **Open on an image that holds the spark, and on pressure.** The first paragraph should
   make the reader see something and want to know something. The threat or opponent
   acts on page one. Fold world rules and logistics into conflict scenes, never into a
   briefing.
2. **Dramatise the key scenes, summarise the connective tissue.** Choose about 8–12
   scenes to render vividly, always including every hinge beat (the choices the
   climax depends on: Gauntlet G9), using the Manual discipline: a visual way in, conflicting wants,
   the turn, and a pull out. Give the rest a sentence or two.
3. **Chain with causality.** Paragraph transitions should read as *therefore* or *but*.
4. **Subtext, briefly.** One interior line per key beat ("She agrees, but only because
   it puts her back inside the investigation"), in the spirit of McKee's
   treatment. Don't narrate every feeling.
5. **Make the stakes visible.** The reader should feel all three stakes without the
   words "external", "internal" or "philosophical" appearing.
6. **Make the values debate dramatic.** Let the antagonist aria and the mentor's
   counter-view happen as events or confrontations. The decisive act proves the idea.
7. **Tone on the page.** A comedy treatment should be funny and a thriller tense. Rhythm,
   word choice and image selection should carry the genre.
8. **Specific over generic.** Use the real place names, objects, customs and textures
   the writer gave you. That is the archetype-not-stereotype test (E4).

## Series and pilots
If the project is a series, the treatment usually becomes a **series
bible / pitch document** plus a **pilot story**. Default split:
- Series engine (what generates episodes), world, tone: about 1 page
- Main characters, each with want, need, and what keeps them in conflict: about 1–2 pages
- Pilot treatment (A/B/C stories, ending on the series' central question): about 4–6 pages
- Season 1 arc (tentpoles across episodes; season climax): about 2–3 pages
- Future seasons: about 0.5 page

Apply the Gauntlet to the pilot as a story, and to the season as a story.

## The market package (`07-package.md`)
1. **Title**, with 2–3 alternatives if the working title is weak.
2. **Showhorse logline** (Snyder test), plus a **tagline** if useful.
3. **Short synopsis** (1 paragraph, about 120 words) and **one-page synopsis**.
4. **Genre and tone**: primary genre, secondary genre, a tone sentence, and the genre promise.
5. **Audience**: who it's for and why they'll come.
6. **Comps**: 2–3 titles with one line each on why they're comparable. Mark them
   "verify". Never invent box office, awards or festival facts.
7. **Why this, why now**: its relevance today.
8. **Why me**: a placeholder for the writer's personal connection. Ask for it, and never invent it.
9. **Format notes**: running time or episode count, language(s), main
   locations, approximate scale (low, mid or high budget signals).
10. **Accepted WEAK items** from the Gauntlet, for the writer's own record. Leave them out of what gets sent.
